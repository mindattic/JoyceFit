[ 05 · the phone number ]

Keep his cell
out of it

One dedicated number for texts and calls, separate from his personal line — so leads never see his real number and he never has to wonder who's texting.

Option A

Google Voice

Free. Ties to a Google account, forwards calls/texts to his phone, keeps a written log of every lead. The easy default — start here.

Option B

Burner

A few dollars a month. Fully separate app and inbox, easier to hand off or kill later. Worth it once this is clearly working.

[ 08 · beyond print — social ]

Where to actually
spend the effort

PlatformWhy / how
InstagramThe main one. Form checks, before/after, client wins, Stories for availability. This is where a prospect goes to check he's real before they text the number.
TikTokPure reach — short clips get shown to strangers who've never heard of him, unlike IG's follower-first algorithm. Same footage as Instagram, just cut shorter and posted more often.
FacebookNeighborhood and building Facebook groups are where an older or more local audience actually asks "anyone know a good trainer?" — a profile and a few group posts cover it. Skip a business Page for now.
Twitter / XLow priority. Fitness clients aren't finding a trainer there. Fine to grab the handle for consistency, not worth posting effort yet.

[ 09 · other ways to grow it ]

  • 01 Google Business Profile — free, and it's what shows up when someone searches "personal trainer near me." Takes ten minutes to claim.
  • 02 Referral discount — a free or half-off session for any client who brings a friend. Word of mouth is the cheapest channel there is.
  • 03 Cross-promotion — a coffee shop, juice bar, or physical therapist willing to swap flyers or a shoutout with a trainer they already trust.
  • 04 Gym floor-time — if he rents time at a gym, ask about leaving cards at the desk or a shared board. Walk-up interest is free.
